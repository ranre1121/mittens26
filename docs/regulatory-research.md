# Kazakhstan Cashier-Video Regulatory Research for a ForteBank Hackathon

**Cut-off date:** 17 October 2026  
**Scope:** Public Kazakhstan regulatory sources only; no hackathon videos, frames, or video-derived descriptions were requested or examined.  
**Purpose:** A decision-support prototype for human review, not an automated legal-determination system.

## Strongest verified findings

The strongest public legal basis is the National Bank’s **Resolution No. 231 of 29 November 2019**, which expressly regulates second-tier banks and therefore applies to ForteBank as a Kazakhstan second-tier bank. Official sources identify ForteBank as a second-tier bank and show that it holds a universal banking licence. The rules under Resolution No. 231 govern receipt, issue, counting, sorting, packing, storage and collection of cash; the act was registered on 3 December 2019 as No. 19680 and entered into force on 1 January 2020.[^1][^2][^3][^4]

The following are the most defensible video-related candidates, in descending order of legal and visual strength:

1. **Cashier leaves while still serving a customer.** Paragraph 22 says: `Кассовому работнику не допускается покидать рабочее место до окончания обслуживания клиента`—the cashier may not leave the workplace before customer service is complete. Video can support a possible-violation flag only if the system sees an active customer-service episode, the cashier actually leaves the defined workplace, and the customer has not yet been served to completion. A brief reach outside the frame, a handoff to another authorized cashier, a completed transaction followed by departure, or a camera blind spot can look similar.[^5]

2. **Cash is received or counted outside the contributing customer’s presence.** Paragraphs 19(2) and 20 require the cashier to accept banknotes with sheet-by-sheet counting and coins by individual pieces, with authenticity/payment-validity checks, and provide that cash is accepted and counted in the client’s presence. A video flag requires an identifiable receipt transaction, continuous temporal coverage from cash handover through counting, visibility of both customer and cashier, and enough detail to distinguish counting from merely moving or arranging cash. One frame is insufficient.[^6][^5]

3. **Cash prepared for issue is counted outside the receiving customer’s presence.** Paragraph 31(4) requires the cashier to count cash prepared for issue in the client’s presence and then issue it to the client. Video can support a possible-violation flag only when the system can establish that the sequence is a customer cash withdrawal, that the counted cash is the cash later issued, and that the client was absent throughout the legally relevant count. Pre-counting inventory before the customer arrives, followed by a compliant count in the customer’s presence, is not equivalent.[^5]

4. **Previously accepted cash remains exposed at the workstation during a later customer transaction.** Paragraph 20 states that only the current client’s cash is at the cashier’s workplace; all previously accepted cash is stored in special key-locking devices at that workplace, such as a metal cabinet, safe, trolley, desk drawer or pedestal. A defensible flag needs a continuous sequence showing completion of client A’s receipt, commencement of client B’s service, and client A’s cash still outside a qualifying closed device. A single visible stack cannot establish whose cash it is or whether the earlier transaction has ended.[^5]

5. **Back-office counting, sorting or packing without a controlling employee present.** Paragraph 42 requires these operations to be performed by a cashier in the presence of a `контролирующий работник`, except for cash receipt and issue operations with a customer. This is visually promising only where organizers can label the room/process as back-office counting, sorting or packing rather than a customer transaction, define “presence,” and map staff roles from external records. The model must not infer that the second visible person is—or is not—the required controlling employee from appearance alone.[^5]

6. **Unescorted access by a person who is not one of those responsible for cash safekeeping.** Paragraph 75 permits entry to a vault or safe room by persons responsible for cash safekeeping and by other persons named in a bank managerial order when accompanied by the responsible persons. Video can support a possible flag only if the doorway is reliably mapped as a `кладовая` or `сейфовая комната`, access logs or role records establish that the entrant is not a designated responsible person, and the observation shows entry without the required escort. Appearance alone cannot establish authorization or staff role.[^7]

7. **A visibly compromised collection bag is opened or counted without the collection worker present.** Paragraph 25 requires a cashier who detects a bag-integrity problem to open and count its contents in the presence of a bank collection-unit worker or collection-organization worker. This requires event-level video showing a clearly compromised bag, the opening/counting sequence, room occupancy, and external confirmation of the other person’s role. A normal bag opening, a tamper-evident seal designed to be broken at receipt, or damage outside camera resolution may look similar.[^5]

8. **Personal items or camera-capable communication devices inside the cash centre.** Resolution No. 231, paragraph 12, as amended by National Bank Resolution No. 71 of 10 November 2025, requires employees’ personal belongings, personal cash and valuables to be stored outside the `кассовый узел`, while expressly allowing medicines, medical devices and communication devices *without* photo/video capability to be brought in. This supports at most a contextual-review flag unless the bank supplies a facility map proving that the scene is inside the cash centre, object detail proves the item is personal and camera-capable, and legal review confirms that the wording should be treated as excluding other communication devices. Mere “phone use” should not be labelled unlawful: the rule concerns what may be brought into the cash centre, not a general behavioral prohibition on using all phones.[^8][^9]

## Legal assessment boundaries

Every output should separate **visual observation** from **legal interpretation**. A suitable finding is: “At 10:14:22–10:14:47, the cashier is outside the marked workstation while a customer remains at the service window and cash remains on the counter; review whether service had ended and whether Resolution No. 231, Rules paragraph 22 applies.” It should not say: “The cashier violated paragraph 22.”

The model must not infer identity, job title, authorization, accounting accuracy, transaction type, document content, ownership of cash, completion of service, or whether an unseen step occurred. Those facts require staff rosters, room maps, transaction records, access-control logs, readable documents, or confirmation from a human reviewer.

For omitted acts, a finding is defensible only if three elements are established:

- **Trigger:** External facts establish that the legal step became due—for example, this was a customer cash receipt, not internal cash preparation.
- **Opportunity:** The recording continuously covers the whole period in which the act should have occurred, including relevant entry/exit points and any permissible alternative location.
- **Non-occurrence:** The camera view and resolution are sufficient to show that no equivalent compliant act occurred; absence from one camera view is not proof of omission.

### Status qualification

The official Adilet entry for Resolution No. 231 confirms the act and registration details but, during this research, displayed a notice that the electronic text was absent or being processed. The operative text was therefore cross-checked against the official amendment acts, including Resolution No. 71 of 2025 and Resolution No. 78 of 2026; the latter is important because it proves that the latest 2026 changes concern coin packing rather than the principal behavioral rules used in the prototype. This is a retrieval limitation, not evidence that Resolution No. 231 is repealed.[^2][^10][^6]

## Best prototype shortlist

### 1. Leaving during service

This is the best first event because the legal text is direct, the action has a clear temporal form, and it can be expressed without identifying money amounts or reading documents. Required organizer metadata are the workstation polygon, customer-zone polygon and an authoritative rule for marking transaction completion. The output should be “possible paragraph-22 review event,” not a violation verdict.[^5]

### 2. Client presence during counts

Incoming and outgoing counts have explicit presence requirements in paragraphs 20 and 31(4). A prototype can track a cash-handover/count/issue sequence and whether the customer remains in the relevant zone, but it must maintain separate event classes for receipt and issue and suppress findings when the start or end of the transaction is outside the recording.[^5]

### 3. Prior cash left exposed

Paragraph 20 provides unusually concrete spatial requirements: the current client’s cash may be at the workplace, while previously accepted cash belongs in specified key-locking devices. This is strong for object tracking across consecutive customers, provided the bank annotates transaction boundaries and storage-device regions; without those annotations it should remain an observation, not a flag.[^5]

### 4. Missing controller in back-office processing

Paragraph 42 creates a clear two-person-presence rule for counting, sorting and packing outside the customer receipt/issue exception. It is suitable for a controlled room with fixed cameras and organizer-supplied role/process labels, but unsuitable for general teller footage where the customer exception or off-camera presence cannot be excluded.[^5]

### 5. Collection-bag inspection sequence

Paragraphs 24, 25, 107 and 108 define visible, ordered bag and seal checks, with a special presence requirement when a compromised bag is opened. This is a good sequence-recognition challenge if footage covers the whole handover, bag condition is resolvable, and organizers provide event type and staff-role metadata; document-content checks should be excluded from automated conclusions.[^7][^5]

### 6. Vault entry with authorization data

Paragraph 75 is strong law but weak video in isolation. It becomes defensible only when synchronized with an access-control system and a roster identifying responsible persons and authorized visitors; the model can then detect “entry without a visible escort” while a human reviews the written authorization and logs.[^7]

### Deferred candidates

Camera-capable devices in the cash centre should be deferred until organizers obtain legal confirmation of paragraph 12’s interpretation and provide reliable room boundaries and device-resolution standards. Detailed packaging, denomination/wear sorting, accounting reconciliation, signature/document checks and machine-feature verification should also be deferred because normal CCTV usually cannot establish the required facts.[^9][^8]

## No applicable public basis found

The research found **no sufficiently specific public commercial-bank rule** supporting automatic violation labels for the following behaviors merely because they are visible:

- **Phone use as such.** Paragraph 12 now distinguishes communication devices by photo/video capability and cash-centre location; it does not establish a universal ban on every phone use in every cashier setting.[^8][^9]
- **Eating, drinking or chewing gum** at a cashier workstation.
- **Touching or handling cash with bare hands**, or failure to wear gloves.
- **Failure to use a specific tray, sponge, finger moistener or counting accessory.** Paragraph 41 imposes a functional equipment requirement for counting/sorting, but no reviewed provision establishes that every visible handling action must use a particular device.[^5]
- **Briefly mixing denominations while actively counting or staging cash.** The rules govern sorting and final packaging/storage, but temporary visual mixing is not itself established as a violation.[^5]
- **Conversation, looking away, posture, speed of work, or “distracted” appearance.** These may be internal-control concerns, but no applicable public legal prohibition was verified.
- **One cashier handling an ordinary customer receipt or issue without a second employee.** Paragraph 42’s controller-presence requirement expressly excludes customer cash receipt and issue operations; special cases may have separate requirements.[^5]
- **Use of hands rather than a machine during every customer transaction.** Paragraphs 19 and 31 prescribe counting and paragraph 41 addresses equipment, but the reviewed text does not justify a computer-vision rule that treats every visible manual touch/count as prohibited.[^6][^5]
- **A particular cash amount visible on the counter.** No universal CCTV-enforceable maximum exposure amount was verified in the reviewed public acts.
- **Opening an ordinary sealed bag.** The legal significance depends on the bag type, process stage, condition and participants; opening alone is not a violation.
- **A door momentarily open.** Resolution No. 14 regulates room construction, entries and access/privacy, but the reviewed provisions do not create a universal event rule that every visible open door is unlawful.[^14]
- **Cashier identity, authorization, accounting correctness, or transaction completion inferred from appearance.** These are facts to be supplied from bank systems or established by human review, not visual attributes.

These are not findings that the conduct is permitted under every internal ForteBank policy. They mean only that no sufficiently specific, applicable **public regulatory basis** was verified for the proposed legal label. A publicly available or organizer-supplied internal procedure may create an operational review rule, but it must be labelled “internal policy,” not Kazakhstan regulatory law.

## Organizer questions

1. **What locations are in scope?** Obtain authoritative floor plans and polygons for each `касса`, `кассовый узел`, cash-count room, vault, safe room, pre-vault area, collection box/loading area and ordinary customer hall.
2. **What is the transaction ground truth?** Provide privacy-approved timestamps for service start, cash handover, count, issue/receipt completion, cancellation and cashier handoff. Without this, paragraph-20/22/31 omission findings are unsafe.
3. **Which footage classes exist?** Separate teller-window video, back-office counting-room video, vault/access video, collection-handover video and vehicle video; different rules and exceptions apply.
4. **Can staff roles be supplied from authoritative records?** Required roles include cashier, controlling employee, head cashier, person responsible for cash safekeeping, authorized visitor, collector, collector-driver and senior collection-team member.
5. **How is “presence” defined operationally?** Legal review should decide whether same room, line of sight, physical proximity or immediate supervisory availability is required for paragraphs 20, 25, 42, 83, 107–108 and 113.
6. **How is “completion of customer service” defined for paragraph 22?** Decide whether it is tied to cash handover, receipt printing, document return, system closure, customer departure or an internal procedure.
7. **Will recordings provide continuous pre-roll/post-roll and all relevant cameras?** Omission findings should be disabled when the observation window does not cover the full opportunity to act.
8. **Which key-locking devices qualify under paragraph 20, and what visual state means “stored”?** The bank should label safes, metal cabinets, trolleys, drawers and pedestals and define whether closed-but-not-visibly-locked is sufficient evidence for the prototype.
9. **Does ForteBank’s legal function interpret amended paragraph 12 as prohibiting camera-capable personal communication devices throughout the cash centre?** Also clarify bank-owned devices, emergency exceptions and medical accessibility accommodations.[^9][^8]
10. **Are collection procedures performed by ForteBank’s own unit or an external licensed collection organization?** Contract structure and actor determine which obligations and internal procedures apply.
11. **What internal procedures may the prototype reference?** Paragraphs 23, 36, 37, 56, 69–70, 80, 100 and 124 leave important details to bank internal documents. Any such rule must be stored separately with title, version, owner, effective date and classification as non-public/internal.[^7][^5]
12. **What privacy, labor, cybersecurity and retention approvals cover employee-video analytics?** These legal domains were outside this cash-operations research and require a separate assessment before production use.
13. **Who makes the final legal assessment and how are findings appealed?** A trained human reviewer should confirm applicability, inspect full footage and external records, and record reasons for confirmation or dismissal.
14. **Which exact legal snapshot will be frozen for judging?** Organizers should publish a dated rule pack because temporary Resolution No. 14 text expires on 1 January 2027 unless further amended.[^14]

## Finding format

Each generated finding should contain these separate fields:

- **Observed event:** Neutral, literal description of visible conduct.
- **Event window:** Start and end timestamp, not only a single timestamp.
- **Evidence frames:** At least one establishing context, one showing the relevant action/omission opportunity and one showing the outcome; include camera ID and crop coordinates.
- **Visibility statement:** What is clearly visible and what is occluded, off-camera or below resolution.
- **Rule candidate:** Document title, issuer, date, number and exact paragraph/subparagraph with official URL.
- **Conditional applicability:** “Applies if this is a customer cash-receipt transaction,” “applies if the room is a vault,” or equivalent.
- **External facts required:** Staff role, authorization, access log, transaction state, bag type, document contents or equipment specification.
- **Alternative explanations:** Normal situations that can appear similar.
- **Assessment level:** `possible-violation flag`, `contextual observation`, or `no useful assessment`.
- **Reviewer disposition:** Confirmed for escalation, dismissed, insufficient evidence or wrong rule/context.

A safe machine-generated phrase is: **“Video supports review of possible inconsistency with [provision], subject to confirmation of [external facts].”** The prototype should never produce “illegal,” “guilty,” “fraud,” “theft,” “unauthorized,” or “violated” from video alone.

## Implementation controls

- Keep the **visual event ontology** independent from the **legal-rule registry**. For example, `person_exits_workstation` is a visual event; paragraph 22 is a conditional legal mapping.
- Store versioned rule metadata: original Russian title, authority, date, number, provision, effective-from/to dates, official URL, applicability, amendment chain and reviewer notes.
- Require rule preconditions before generating a flag. If `transaction_type`, `room_type`, `staff_role` or `observation_complete` is unknown, downgrade to context or suppress.
- Treat absence detection as a high-risk operation. The system must establish the due step, the complete opportunity window and sufficient camera coverage.
- Do not use face recognition to infer identity or authority unless a separate lawful, organizer-approved system supplies identity; even then, expose the external source rather than presenting identity as a visual conclusion.
- Do not OCR sensitive customer documents or secret access codes for this use case. Document-content compliance should be reviewed in source banking systems.
- Preserve the original clip around every event, not only selected frames. Still frames are evidence aids but cannot replace temporal context for most shortlisted rules.
- Revalidate the registry after any National Bank amendment and before deployment after 1 January 2027, when temporary wording under Resolution No. 14 is scheduled to end unless changed.[^14]
